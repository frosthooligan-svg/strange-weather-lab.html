4. Grammar — What happened first?

Past Simple: a finished event in the past.
Past Perfect: had + V3 — an earlier completed event.
Past Perfect Continuous: had been + V-ing — an earlier activity in progress / duration.

5. Reading — The storm that changed the afternoon

Read the original text. It uses vocabulary from this lesson and vocabulary from our previous disaster/weather work.

On Saturday afternoon, Maya and her family were visiting a small coastal town. The weather forecast had promised sunshine, but by midday the sky had become dark and overcast. Strong winds had been blowing for almost an hour when the first hailstones hit the windows.

Maya had never seen weather like that before. A powerful storm moved across the coast, and a lightning bolt struck a tree near the road. Luckily, nobody was hurt. The emergency services arrived quickly because several residents had called for help.

By the time the family reached a safe building, the rain had become extremely heavy. Water was rising in the streets, so the police told people to stay indoors. Maya later learned that the town had experienced floods before, but this storm had caused much more damage.

When the storm finally passed, the residents came outside. The streets were covered with branches and mud. Everyone was in bad shape, but there was a silver lining: neighbours worked together to clean the streets and restore damaged homes. Maya was astonished by how quickly people had started to help.

7. Final challenge — Be the weather reporter

Mission: Imagine a strange weather event has just happened in your town. Speak for 60–90 seconds.

You must use:

  • at least 3 new words
  • at least 2 old weather/disaster words
  • one Past Perfect sentence
  • one Past Perfect Continuous sentence
  • one idiom

Useful opening: “Good evening. We are in the centre of the town, where a very unusual weather event has just happened…”

Good evening. We are in the centre of the town, where a very unusual storm has just happened. The weather forecast had predicted light rain, but strong winds had been blowing since the morning. By the time the storm arrived, the residents had already moved to safe buildings. Hailstones covered the streets, and one lightning bolt struck a tree. The emergency services worked quickly, although they had struggled to cope with the amount of damage. Everyone is exhausted and a little under the weather, but every cloud has a silver lining: neighbours are helping to restore the damaged houses.
